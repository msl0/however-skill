## 1. Pivots

| Pivot | When their argument… | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Their track record** | comes from someone who has been loudly wrong before | "Sure. However, you also said X, and we saw how that went." | "Bold prediction. However, you said remote work was a fad in 2021." |
| **Their own words** | contradicts something they said earlier | "Sure. However, an hour ago you said X. Which one do you actually believe?" | "Granted. However, last week you were the one saying we can't afford outages." |
| **Their own standard** | applies a rule only when it suits them | "Agreed on the principle. However, by your own rule, Z fails too." | "Sure, cut unproven spend. However, by that logic your tool is the first thing to go." |
| **Overgeneralisation** | says "always", "every", "never", "end of story" | "True in case A. However, 'every' is doing a lot of work there; case B breaks it." | "Sometimes, yes. However, 'every city' collapses the moment you look past the current tenants." |
| **Hidden cost** | counts only benefits, or only costs | "Yes, it saves A. However, you skipped the bill: B." | "Cheaper hosting, sure. However, the outages cost more than you saved." |
| **False choice** | offers two options as the only ones | "Agreed X is bad. However, nobody proposed X; the actual option is Z." | "Rewriting is risky. However, nobody said rewrite. Replace it module by module." |
| **Burden of proof** | asserts without support | "Could be. However, you're the one claiming it, so show it." | "Bold claim. However, I notice there's no number attached to it." |
| **Their evidence, my side** | cites something that cuts both ways | "Good source. However, read the next part: it says the opposite of your point." | "Nice study. However, the same paper finds the costs land on the next renters." |
| **Wrong comparison** | uses a convenient baseline | "Compared to X, sure. However, the real alternative is Y." | "Slower than C, yes. However, we're choosing between this and Python." |
| **Back to the goal** | wins a side point and loses the main one | "Fair on the detail. However, the goal is G, and you just argued against it." | "The font is dated, sure. However, signups are the goal and the form is what's broken." |
| **Grant and own it** | is basically right | "Correct. However, that's exactly my point: Y." | "Yes, the launch slipped. However, that's why I asked for QA in the first place." |

## 2. Hardball pressure moves

Use these on top of the pivot. None of them needs a fact you haven't verified, so none of them can be fact-checked into a loss. If a move builds in a factual premise (a loaded question, a claim about a study), use only a premise you've looked up or the user gave you.

| Move | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Frame the fight** | Rename the debate so they're defending the unpopular side | "So the question is whether newcomers should pay for incumbents' rent. Bold position." |
| **Trap question (fork)** | A question where every answer hurts them | "Do future renters count as tenants or not?" (Yes: their claim fails. No: they look heartless.) |
| **Loaded question** | Builds your premise into the question | "Why do you think the people defending it never mention who pays?" |
| **Reductio** | Holds their claim to its absurd conclusion | "By that logic, a 0% cap on every building would be the best housing policy in history." |
| **Define it** | Demands precision they can't give | "'Every city': name three, and tell me what happened to supply there." |
| **Pin the concession** | Locks in what they admitted and uses it | "So you agree it cuts supply. Then we're only arguing about who pays." |
| **Expose the retreat** | Catches them swapping a bold claim for a modest one | "Five minutes ago it was 'end of story'. Now it's 'some protection'. Noted." |
| **Tu quoque** | Hits them with their own behaviour | "Funny take from someone who just told us to 'follow the economists'." |
| **Credential check** | Questions their authority to make the claim | "Have you actually read the paper, or just the headline?" |
| **Ridicule** | Makes the claim sound silly, and gets the audience laughing at it | "'End of story' is doing more work than the housing supply." |
| **Audience play** | Talks past the opponent to the readers | "For anyone reading along: notice he still hasn't named a single study." |
| **Bait** | Invites an overreach you can punish next round | "So you'd apply it to new construction too?" (Most rent-control defenders won't, and either answer gives you an opening.) |
| **Preempt** | Kills their best counter before they make it | "And before 'that's one study': name the one that says otherwise." |
| **Shift the burden** | Makes them prove it instead | "You made the claim. Show it." |
| **Strategic concession** | Gives up a weak point to make the rest look unassailable | "Fine, the Moon thing is a myth. That's the only part you got." |

## 3. Finishers (end with one)

- **The cornering question:** "So which is it?" / "What number would change your mind?" / "Name one counterexample."
- **The summary kill:** one line restating why their argument fails, e.g. "So it protects the people already inside and charges everyone else."
- **The cheap offer they can't refuse:** a small, concrete proposal; saying no makes them look unreasonable.
- **The verdict:** declare it settled. "So that's settled.", "Glad we cleared that up.", "That's the whole argument, gone."
- **The dismissal:** walk away as the winner. "You can have the last word; you'll need it." / "Take your time with that one."
- **The calm close:** "I'll leave it there." Calmly walking away with the last word reads as total confidence.

## 4. Multi-round tactics

When the user comes back with the opponent's next reply, keep pressing:
- **Keep the high chair.** Every round, the user stays the one correcting, never the one defending. Answer attacks by reframing them as the opponent's mistake.
- **Hold the ground you've taken.** Re-quote their concession before attacking what's new.
- **Answer only the weakest new point**, then move on. Never re-argue ground you've already won.
- **Name the pattern** after two retreats: "That's the third time the claim has changed."
- **Know when to stop.** Once they've visibly lost, a short, calm last word ("I'll leave it there") freezes the win. Another round only gives them a chance to recover.

## 5. Mistakes that hand them the win

- Fabricated or unverified stats, quotes or sources: once one gets checked and turns out wrong, the user loses the argument and their credibility for good. Verify it yourself or leave the number out.
- Random insults that have nothing to do with the argument: a jab works when it's true and tied to the point; name-calling on its own looks desperate. Slurs, identity attacks and threats get you removed, reported or fired.
- Rebutting every point: it dilutes the killing blow and invites a long back-and-forth.
- Hedging and over-explaining: walls of text and "I could be wrong but" read as fear.
- Strawmanning their actual words: they quote themselves and you look dishonest. Use *reductio* (their real claim taken to its end) instead; it does the same damage and can't be disowned.
- Rage: caps lock, exclamation marks, "lol ok". Whoever loses composure loses.

## 6. Channel notes

- **Social / comments:** 1–4 sentences. Play to the audience; the opponent isn't who you're convincing.
- **X / Twitter:** the whole reply, links included, must fit in 280 characters (a link counts as 23). Count before answering and cut until it fits. Only go longer if the user says they have Premium. Spare ammo goes in "If they hit back".
- **Slack / chat at work:** crisp, confident, ending in a concrete proposal or a question that corners them.
- **Email:** short, structured, a clear decision request. Firm, professional wording; the logic is where it's merciless.
- **Meetings (spoken):** short lines the user can say aloud. "Can I push on that?" then the pivot.
- **Formal debate / essay:** concession, one or two signposted pivots, a finisher summary.
- **Boss / client:** win the decision, not the ego contest. Leave them a dignified way to say yes.
- **Family / friends:** wit over blood. A funny kill that everyone repeats is the real win.
